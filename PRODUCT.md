# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Mexican DJs, music producers, and event planners who need specialized DJ furniture and curation services. They browse on desktop for detailed purchasing and use mobile for quick looks and bookings between gigs. The audience values authenticity, craftsmanship, and a distinct cultural identity rooted in Mexico's music scene.

## Product Purpose

GDL. is an e-commerce and service booking platform for the DJ/producer industry. It sells specialized furniture (DJ tables, production gear) and curates sonic experiences for events. "Música que se Siente" — music you can feel. Success means becoming the go-to brand for DJ craftsmanship in Mexico.

## Brand Personality

Raw, Passionate, Premium. The voice is confident but not arrogant, knowledgeable but not exclusive. Tone is direct, tactile, grounded in the physical reality of DJ culture — gear, wood, vinyl, bass. Emotional goal: make the user feel like they're part of a scene that knows what's good.

## Anti-references

Avoid anything that looks like:
- Generic SaaS or corporate — Bootstrap blues, rounded corners everywhere, stock photography of smiling people, modern sans-serif minimalism.
- Cheap templated ecommerce — Shopify defaults, WooCommerce cookie-cutter storefronts.
- Overdesigned noisy chaos — Too many gradients, animations, or effects that feel like a 2010s music site.
- GDL. should feel unique, intentional, and rooted in the physical craft of music production.

## Design Principles

1. **Rhythm in everything.** Spacing, scroll, composition should have a beat like music. The layout breathes and pulses — tight groupings, generous separations, intentional pauses.
2. **Raw confidence.** No polish for polish's sake. Let materials speak — heavy serifs, monospace body, dark surfaces, a single neon accent. The interface feels tactile, like gear you can touch.
3. **Premium without pretense.** High-quality feel that stays accessible to working DJs. The aesthetic is elevated but not exclusive. Craftsmanship over expense.
4. **Physical meets digital.** The 3D model, the border-heavy industrial layout, the weight of typography — the digital should feel as real as a turntable or a wooden DJ booth.
5. **One accent, one voice.** Magenta is the only color that deviates from neutral. Use it sparingly so it hits. Every other choice serves the monochrome industrial palette.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard best practices — semantic HTML, keyboard navigation, sufficient contrast. No formal WCAG target, but the dark theme with high-contrast text (off-white on near-black) is inherently legible. The magenta accent on dark backgrounds needs careful chroma management to stay readable.
